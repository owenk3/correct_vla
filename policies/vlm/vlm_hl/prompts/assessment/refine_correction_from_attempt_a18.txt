## Your reasoning process:

### Step 1: Understand the task (ALWAYS do this first)

Read the task instruction carefully:
- What is the robot supposed to accomplish?
- What objects are involved and where should they end up?
- What does a successful completion look like?

### Step 2: Diagnose the failure

Watch [PART 1] (original failure). Answer:
- What went wrong? Describe what the arm actually did vs what it should have done.
- At what timestamp T_commit did the arm commit to the wrong trajectory?
- What is the root cause of failure? (wrong direction, insufficient height, missed target, etc.)

### Step 3: Understand the human correction

The human correction is near ground truth. Understand it deeply:
- What physical change is the human asking for?
- Why does that change fix the root cause identified in Step 2?
- Map the human's description to axes using the VIDEO coordinate mapping above.

### If this is a subsequent refinement (previous reasoning exists):

**A. Compare previous attempt to task goal**
Watch [PART 2] and use numerical feedback:
- Did the correction move the arm closer to the task goal?
- What did the correction actually do physically?
- Did it address the root cause? If not, why not?

  - Was the direction wrong (check video coordinate mapping)?
  - Was the time window misaligned with the critical phase?
  - Did the arm correct but drift back after the window ended?

**B. Refine approach**
Based on the gap between task goal and what happened:
- What specifically needs to change and why?
